WIPO Africa Intellectual Property Analysis:

About the Project:

As I learned more about WIPO, its mission, and intellectual property, I developed an interest in understanding how intellectual property applications are distributed across Africa. I chose to focus on Africa because, as an African resident myself, I had very limited knowledge of intellectual property until recently. I found it interesting to discover how a concept that I had barely encountered could have such a wide global impact.

The project explores intellectual property application trends across African countries between 2018 and 2024, focusing on patents, trademarks, and industrial designs.

Dataset and Tools:

The data used comes from the WIPO Statistics Data Center. The indicator analyzed is "applications via the direct and Madrid systems", covering 3 forms of intellectual property: trademarks, industrial designs, and patents, over the period from 2018 to 2024.

I used MySQL to transform the raw data and filter the dataset to African countries. I then used Power BI to build a dashboard and visualize the data to identify and showcase insights.

What I Found:

Trademark applications were the most common form of intellectual property protection among the top 10 African countries by application volume, with Algeria leading the ranking. Industrial design applications came second, while patent applications were the smallest category among the three. The main filing route was through direct national offices rather than international filing systems.

Over the 6-year period, applications generally increased, with a noticeable peak between 2023 and 2024. This increase raises an interesting question for further investigation: could the growth of AI, digital creation, entrepreneurship, and other forms of innovation be among the factors contributing to the increase in intellectual property applications?

Conclusion:

The analysis shows that intellectual property application activity is present across Africa, while also revealing significant differences between countries and types of intellectual property protection. Initiatives such as WIPO's 1% Project in Nigeria represent an opportunity to further expand intellectual property awareness among African youth, entrepreneurs, and creatives, helping them understand how IP protection can support their work and ideas.

This project started with a simple curiosity about IP activity in Africa and led me to explore WIPO data through SQL and Power BI. It allowed me to turn that curiosity into a practical analysis while developing my skills in SQL data preparation, data analysis, and Power BI visualization.
